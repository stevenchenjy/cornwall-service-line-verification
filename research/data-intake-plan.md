Ask how assignments were chosen. Distinguish differences in case difficulty from method performance. ### Target quantities and admission checks

The target comparison is additional distinct newly accepted complete-line classifications under the same actual staff-time budget and horizon as current practice, with agreed coverage constraints. A correction to an already accepted case is separate from a newly classified case. Report starting stock, newly accepted cases, reversals/corrections and unresolved follow-up separately; never infer period classifications by subtracting unrelated stock totals.

For a defined initial/evolving state and prior action history, an action transition probability would use the number of observed eligible attempted events entering a specified outcome/state divided by **all** such eligible attempts, including no response, inconclusive work, rejection and deferral. Specify the observation window and handle unfinished follow-up/censoring explicitly. Submitted evidence, accepted side evidence and accepted final category are separate endpoints. Repeated events within one line are dependent; the event denominator is not a count of independent lines. Report joint public/customer-side outcomes directly, without multiplying marginal side rates. Neither side counts nor inventory method frequencies reconstruct a sequence transition table.

Resource cost is observed staff person-time for the same actions, including failures, preparation, travel, contact/field work and administration/review. Sum disjoint measured components and count shared travel/review once under an explicit allocation rule. Report missing components and the population with complete measurement; do not extrapolate that subset silently. Missing total cost blocks the affected budget comparison even if outcomes are observed. Cost in money additionally requires dated unit-cost evidence; elapsed time alone does not supply worker time.

Before fitting or comparing sequences, the lead and authorized/human reviewers must check:

1. Dated system membership/unique-line baseline, source coverage and applicable category-specific rules, including historical conditions and representation limits.
2. Eligibility at each action, actual sequence/order, initial difficulty, prior failures, submitted/accepted evidence and joint outcomes, plus complete denominators or explicitly bounded missing capture.
3. Current practice as actually performed, its queue/retry/escalation rules and the same horizon, staff budget/capacity and coverage priorities for every candidate sequence.
4. Supported action/state/history overlap and selection mechanisms. Complete records alone do not identify a causal policy effect. Any adjustment requires defensible measured-confounding, sequential-selection, positivity and stability assumptions, with their untestable limits stated. Without support, restrict results to observed groups and descriptive associations.
5. Uncertainty and dependence, sparse cells, missingness/censoring, shared time, hard-to-reach cases and coverage exclusions. Obtain approved fairness/coverage constraints before any allocation recommendation; do not infer sensitive household traits or silently optimize only the easiest cases.
